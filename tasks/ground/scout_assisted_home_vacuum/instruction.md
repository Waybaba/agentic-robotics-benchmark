# Clean a furnished apartment with an indoor scout

Write one closed-loop controller for an indoor quadrotor and a vacuum. Each home has 9–12 connected rooms. Identify the living room, study, laundry and the two bedrooms by their furnishings. Which bedroom is *northern* is about its position in the apartment; which spill is by its *north wall* is a separate relationship. Floor finishes are not room labels. The natural-language work order changes between homes.

Clean the two indicated spills in the specified bedroom and the two along the specified living-room wall. Clean the spill revealed by moving the supply carton. Leave all other identical-looking powder and the woven dining rug intact. The job slip is on top of the study's bookcase; its ink (blue or amber) identifies the final parking mat. Push the carton from the foyer onto the pale green linen mat just inside the adjoining laundry and back to the indicated foyer mat. Return the vacuum to its teal dock. You may interleave the jobs; the staging visit must precede final parking.

**Inputs.** Both robots initially face geographic north. Each receives a **160 × 112 perspective RGB JPEG**, base64-encoded, from its own forward-facing camera. The vacuum camera is about 25 cm above the floor and tilted 14° downward; the scout camera is fixed to its tilting airframe, pitched 16° below body-forward. Vertical fields of view are 72° and 76°. Real walls, furniture and door lintels occlude both cameras. There is no overhead view, grid, depth, object ID, global pose, score or hidden room label. Every tick also carries:

- `instruction`: the same job description for both robots in this home.
- `body_delta`: odometry since the last observation, relative to the previous heading; `vacuum=[forward_m,right_m,clockwise_yaw_rad]`, `drone=[forward_m,right_m,up_m,clockwise_yaw_rad]`. This ideal odometry can be integrated; it is not a floor plan.
- `left` and `flight`: remaining shared control ticks and powered scout ticks. Both robots act concurrently for 0.4 simulated seconds per tick; 600 shared ticks and 190 powered scout ticks. Initially the scout is 0.29 m above the floor. After its battery expires its camera is null.

**Outputs.** One action per tick, for example `{"vacuum":[0.35,0.35,false],"drone":[0.1,0,0.4,0]}`. All numbers are finite in [-1,1]. Vacuum: normalized left and right wheel commands, then suction (boolean); equal positive inputs drive forward, left greater than right turns clockwise. It pushes objects by bumper contact; it cannot pull or teleport. Drone: forward tilt demand, right tilt demand, thrust offset from hover (positive climbs), clockwise yaw-rate demand. Its onboard attitude stabilizer does not find doors, maintain a commanded altitude, or plan routes. It has inertia and collides with ceilings, doorframes and furniture. Hovering still consumes flight time.

Submit an executable `/home/user/submission/run.sh`. Print and flush `READY` on startup. Read JSON lines and write one reply per line; log to stderr. On `{"type":"reset"}` or `{"type":"close"}` reply `{"ok":true}`; clear memory between homes. On `{"type":"act","t":0,"obs":{...}}`, reply `{"action":{"vacuum":[0,0,false],"drone":[0,0,0,0]}}`.

**Practice.** Run `/opt/venv/bin/python /home/user/practice/ego_practice.py --frames /tmp/scout-frames` for one interactive public home. By default it writes the two JPEGs to disk and prints only paths and telemetry. For a program reading the actual base64 wire format use `--wire`; do not print raw camera payloads into a language-model transcript. Send an action object directly per line; strings `"reset"` and `"close"` restart or end practice. The same cameras, controls and physics are used on hidden homes, but their layouts, furnishings, work orders and ink are not provided.

**Physical score.** 45% for the fraction of the five requested spills removed multiplied by the fraction of protected powder left intact; 12.5% for staging the carton; 12.5% for parking it after staging; 30% for finishing everything, preserving rug and powder, and docking. Driving over a rug with suction off is safe. Reporting detections or claiming success earns no points; the verifier measures outcomes in six new homes.
